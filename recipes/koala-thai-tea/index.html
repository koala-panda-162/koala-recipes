Koala Thai Tea 🐨🧋

🧋

Bold tea.
One whole can.
No evaporated milk.

A bolder, sweeter variation of Thai iced tea, adapted from the Hot Thai Kitchen inspiration recipe. This is your chosen custom ratio, not a tested original from that source.

≈ 8–10 servings with ice10-minute off-heat steepNo evaporated milkVery strong tea

Ingredients

Optional: Add unsweetened soy milk by the glass if you want it creamier. The base recipe does not use evaporated milk.

Method

  1. Heat the water: Bring 8 cups (≈ 1.9 L) water just to a boil. Turn off the heat.
  2. Steep: Stir in 2 level, loosely filled cups of Thai tea leaves, making sure all the leaves become wet. Cover and steep for 10 minutes off the heat, then strain. Do not keep the tea boiling during the steep. This is an unusually high amount of tea, deliberately chosen for a very strong brew.
  3. Strain well: Pour through a fine-mesh strainer or tea filter, removing all tea leaves. Do not squeeze aggressively; that can add bitterness.
  4. Sweeten: While the brewed tea is still warm, stir in the entire 14 oz can of sweetened condensed milk and ¼ tsp salt until completely dissolved.
  5. Chill: Refrigerate the tea until thoroughly cold.
  6. Serve: Pour over ice. Add unsweetened soy milk to individual glasses only if desired.

Strength note: Two cups of dry tea for eight cups of water is a very strong tea-to-water ratio. The 10-minute steep is your chosen starting point for this strong recipe, not a tested optimal time. Long steeping can increase astringency; if a future batch tastes too bitter, reduce the steep time. Taste again after chilling and dilute with cold water or extra ice if needed. The final strained tea yield will be lower than 8 cups because leaves absorb liquid.

Recipe journal: Named Koala Thai Tea on November 26, 2026. This version uses your specified 2 cups of tea leaves, 8 cups of water, one whole can of condensed milk, no evaporated milk, and a 10-minute off-heat steep. It is not yet recorded as a completed, taste-tested batch.
